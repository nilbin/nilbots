## Method

**Metric.** Signed territorial delta (mine − theirs) per match, plus win-loss-draw.
Territory is the mode's own tiebreak and it saturates at ±60 (a base breach), so
the record is reported beside it everywhere.

**Cells.** The commissioned striker cell set on the `swell` game, spelled
`--classes <pair> --movement facing-locked --pendulum keel --skills kit --bend
universal --aim offset --stance-ground open --cooldown ticking --volley salvo`,
with `--five-slots wane` added in the fabricator pair. Resolved ruleset:
`frontline-labs-1-striker-vs-striker-swell-facing-locked` (rules fingerprint
`b433356e692941ae08062fe5be2c0d2facf208f81162aabb152233c41c4fc3c5`).

**Pairings.** 8 opponents × 20 seeds. Same-class cells (the three strikers) are
played from **both team sides**, so each striker opponent contributes 40 matches
and the cell set is 220 matches per configuration.

**Seeds are not observations.** Both policies are deterministic; the seed feeds
`context.Random`, which this lineage uses only to order equal-scoring directions.
Seeds therefore produce distinct *replays* but frequently identical *outcomes*.
Disclosed for the shipped configuration over its 220 matches: **220/220 distinct
replay hashes, 41 distinct (opponent, outcome, territory) triples.** Sixty of
those matches (the whole bulwark cell) collapse to 3 distinct triples. Nothing
here should be read as 220 independent trials.

**Statistic.** Every configuration plays the identical 220 pairings, so all
comparisons are **paired**: the reported number is the mean per-pairing
difference in territorial delta and its standard error over the 220 pairings, and
the count of pairings whose result actually changed. A configuration that changes
2 pairings out of 220 is reported as such, whatever its point estimate.

**The team-side bias is real and is cancelled, not credited.** On this
mirror-symmetric map, the wave-6 artifact playing *itself* scores +22.0 mean
territory when it is `--bot` (team 0), across 12 seeds. Every same-class pairing
is therefore played from both sides; with that correction the self-mirror comes
out at exactly **+0.00 ± 6.78 over 40 matches, 18-18-4**, which is the sanity
check the correction has to pass. An earlier 12-seed one-sided run of this same
comparison would have credited the revision ~+22 that belongs to the team slot.

**Search versus attribution.** Candidate rules were found by screening each rule
alone against a null configuration (build-up), because I needed to know which
ideas existed before I could have a working whole. The **reported attribution is
leave-one-out from the shipped whole**, as required; rules that are not shipped
are reported as add-one-in cost against that same whole, which is the only honest
way to price something that is off.

**The null is exact.** With all seven switches off, the artifact reproduces the
wave-6 result to the digit — 69-146-5 at −18.80 with 325 entries and 321 casts —
so the switch scaffolding is a faithful identity and every measured difference
below is attributable to a rule rather than to the refactor.

### Reading of each number

- **`RepricedBar` is the pass.** It carries +7.01 ± 1.51, and +35.15 ± 5.81 of
  that is the fabricator cell alone (spark-line goes 0-20 → 20-0). In the striker
  cell it is +1.13 ± 1.14 — a point estimate inside its own error bar. I claim the
  fabricator result and I do not claim the striker one.

- **`EntryClockRead`, `GuardAwareFan`, `ClockTempo` measure exactly zero on 0 of
  220 pairings, and each has a different reason.**
  - *Entry clock:* the legality mask already refuses a route inside its cooldown.
    Measured directly: on 420 mobile life-ticks in the sample where
    `self.routeCooldowns` named the entry as live, `transform` came back
    `available: false` every time. A mask-driven bot is correct for free; the
    explicit read is a belt on braces and a hedge against a contract where the
    two diverge.
  - *Guard-aware fan:* the bulwark cohort raises the aegis shell often (480 shell
    entries across the bulwark cell in the shipped sweep), but the cast ledger's
    other gates never coincide with a shell whose facing quadrant one of my rays
    would enter. The rule is correct and inert here. Note the arithmetic it must
    survive: at 3 health and a doubled bolt, ONE deflected ray never returns
    lethal damage, so the hard refusal only ever binds on two guarded bodies at
    once. The graded penalty was added for that reason and still measured zero.
  - *Clock tempo (hold the gun for a stance about to reopen):* the condition
    requires an enemy visible, a low-value shot available, and the clock within
    one tick of opening. It never co-occurred.
- **`StanceCommit` measures −0.19 ± 0.19 on 1 of 220 pairings.** It converts
  3 would-be `mobilize` exits into fired fans across 220 matches. It is shipped
  because the contract argument is airtight — firing spends the same exit windup
  a requested return spends, and the stance gun's cooldown is at or below the
  mobile gun's, so leaving loaded costs exactly what shooting costs and
  additionally throws away the entry clock — and because its measured cost is
  indistinguishable from zero. It is not a win and is not reported as one.
- **`HedgeFan` (cast for a single body that is still choosing its tile and that
  two rays cover) is the pass's biggest failure**, at −11.46 ± 2.59 across 92
  changed pairings. It nearly triples stance entries and loses 25 more matches.
  The theory — three simultaneous lanes are the answer to a body that has not
  committed to one — is exactly backwards for a *territorial* doctrine: the fan
  buys damage, not ground, and the extra damage (9 659 dealt against 9 172 taken,
  versus 8 193/7 934 for the whole) is proportional on both sides. It also loses
  8.40 of territory in the bulwark cell, the only rule that moves that cell at all.
- **`CheapEntry` (price the blind windup by its declared length instead of by the
  existence of a loaded enemy gun) costs 5.39 ± 2.70.** Revision 6's clause
  refused any tile a ready enemy muzzle *could* cover, which on a widened envelope
  is most of the ground in front of you, and I expected removing it to be the
  unlock. It is not: casting from under a loaded gun is punished, and the wide
  refusal was doing real work despite its stated rationale being wrong.
- **`KillThreshold` is the interesting null.** −0.24 ± 1.72 with 72 pairings
  changed and a worse record: it fires constantly and converts nothing. The reason
  is that the threshold was *already* in the valuation —
  `EnemyForecast.Pressure(damagePerHit)` has taken the firing profile's own damage
  since revision 5, so the doubled bolt moved the kill threshold by itself. An
  additional structural override on top double-counts it.
- **`CastStagger` (defer to a better-ranked sibling whose published clock is
  open) is the honest near-miss:** −0.43 ± 0.22 with an *identical* 91-125-4
  record and 7 pairings changed. Two standard errors of a very small number
  resting on seven matches is thinner than any margin this lineage has claimed
  before, so it does not ship. It is the only rule that used the ALLY clock, and
  its being the only near-miss rather than a win is the honest answer to "can the
  ally clock be scheduled around": on this cell set, at these body counts, not
  measurably.

## What did not work and why it is worth recording

My first working whole turned every idea on at once. It measured **17-78-1 at
−33.04** over 96 matches — decisively worse than the predecessor's −23.38 on the
same set. Leave-one-out from that whole showed four of seven rules individually
*negative*, which is what a whole assembled from plausible theory rather than
from measurement looks like. Two structural errors were behind it:

1. **I conflated ideas inside single switches.** `RepricedBar` originally carried
   both the bar formula and the entry-exposure re-pricing; `SingleBodyFan` carried
   both the kill threshold and the lane hedge. Splitting them (into `RepricedBar` /
   `CheapEntry` and `KillThreshold` / `HedgeFan`) turned an uninterpretable −8.97
   into a +7.01 and a −5.39, and a +7.88 and a −11.46. A switch that bundles two
   ideas cannot be attributed, and leave-one-out will happily report a confident
   number for the bundle.
2. **I assumed "the fan got better" meant "cast more".** It does not. Entries went
   46 → 199 in the first whole and territory got *worse*. The re-armed fan is a
   damage verb; this doctrine scores territory; damage that does not convert to
   ground is noise on both sides of the ledger. The shipped pass raises entries by
   35%, not by 330%.

## Platform friction

1. **A cooldown-held route still advertises its target form.** While
   `self.routeCooldowns` holds `volley-striker-prime` shut, the `transform`
   legality is correctly `available: false` — but its `form-target` constraint
   *still lists* `striker-prime-volley-stance` in `allowedFormIds`. Reading the
   argument domain without reading `Available` yields a refused request every tick
   of the window. Everything the docs say points the right way ("an ordinary
   Blocked"), but the observed shape is "available false, domain unchanged", and
   that combination is not stated anywhere.
2. **`EXPERIMENTAL-FRONTLINE-CLASSES.md` still describes the volley's windup as
   2** in the `--skills` table ("windup **2** into an immobile stance whose gun
   fires **three simultaneous damage-1 bolts**"), and the Volley-salvo section
   below corrects both numbers. The doc does tell you to read
   `windup.durationTicks` rather than assume, and the identity re-mint from `surf`
   to `swell` is explained — but the table and the section disagree on the same
   page, and the table is the part a reader skims.
3. **The one-tick entry has no wait-only tick, and that is not obvious from the
   grammar.** `completion: end-of-started-tick-plus-duration-minus-one` with
   duration 1 means the transition completes at the end of the tick it started, so
   `BlindWindup` (which is true — `pendingAction: wait-only`) describes a window
   that never contains a decision. Priced naively, that is a whole phantom tick of
   exposure. I only found it by reading a replay tick by tick.
4. **`--print-candidate-contract` prints the identity, not the contract.** It
   emits 811 bytes of fingerprints. The only way to read the resolved contract a
   bot will actually see is to run a match and pull `header.contract` out of the
   replay. For a pass whose entire job is re-reading four declared numbers, that
   is the difference between a two-second check and a match.
5. **`--classes` is mandatory for `.wasm` specs, silently.** A declared class in
   `botarena.json` binds the canonical team side automatically, but a bare
   artifact carries no manifest, so `--bot me --opponent bulwark --classes
   bulwark-vs-striker` quietly runs *me* as the bulwark. Nothing warns. The
   cross-class sweeps had to fix the side per opponent.
6. **A frozen tree's `ProjectReference` depth is load-bearing for local editing.**
   `nilbots build` ignores the csproj, but `dotnet build` does not, so copying a
   frozen tree to a scratch directory at a different depth silently breaks the
   editing loop while the WASM build keeps working. I restructured my scratch to
   match the freeze depth rather than edit the reference.
7. **Mirror cells have a team-side bias large enough to be mistaken for a
   result.** +22.0 mean territory for team 0 in a self-mirror on a symmetric map.
   Nothing in the packet or the rules card mentions this; it is a consequence of
   the two sides' distinct `context.Random` streams meeting a doctrine that uses
   the stream to break directional ties. Any same-class measurement that does not
   play both sides is measuring the slot.
8. **`qualify` writes viewers even though viewers are opt-in everywhere else.**
   `nilbots experiment frontline-labs` writes no `viewer.html` without `--viewer`
   or `--open`, but the qualification suite emits one per probe replay — 36 of
   them, ~5 MB each. My raw `evidence/t4` came out at 212 MB against 20 MB of
   actual evidence. This freeze prunes them, keeping the 3 `qualification.json`
   and 37 `replay.json` that wave 6 kept (38 MB total). A sweeping author who
   qualifies several candidates will fill a disk without being told why.
9. **Replay volume is the real disk cost of a measured pass.** 3 852 matches
   produced 40 GB of `replay.json`, because a 500-tick replay v3 embeds the exact
   observation handed to every life on every tick. That is exactly what makes the
   analysis possible, so this is a note rather than a complaint — but a sweep
   script needs to delete as it goes, and mine did not until the end.

## Carried-forward friction (still true from wave 6)

- Debug strings are part of the decision and therefore part of the replay hash.
  Adding a diagnostic to a cast's reason string changes 220 replay hashes while
  changing zero outcomes. Useful, but it means "did the hash change" is not a
  behaviour test.
- The qualification suite runs the duel-depth **union** profile, not the class
  arm, so a class-armed bot's T4 says nothing about its class play. Both were
  measured separately here.
