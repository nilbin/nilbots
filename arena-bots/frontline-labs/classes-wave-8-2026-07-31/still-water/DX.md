## Platform friction

1. **A replay hash can never be equal across two artifacts, so "distinct replay
   hashes" is not evidence of anything.** `header.provenance` embeds each
   participant's NAME and artifact SHA-256, and the canonical hash covers the
   header. My wave-8 null configuration — every new switch off — reproduces the
   wave-7 artifact's behaviour exactly on all 220 paired matches I checked
   (identical outcome, identical territory, identical stance entries, identical
   casts) and shares **zero** replay hashes with it. Wave 7's DX reported
   "220/220 distinct replay hashes" as a disclosure of seed independence; that
   number was always going to be 220/220, whatever the bots did. The honest
   statistic is distinct *outcome tuples*, which this DX reports instead.
2. **Two masks in the same catalog disagree about what an unavailable action's
   argument domain means.** `invest` empties it: when the bank cannot afford any
   tier the legality is `available: false` **and** its `upgrade-track`
   constraint carries `allowedTrackIds: []`. `transform` does not: inside a
   route cooldown the legality is `available: false` while its `form-target`
   constraint still lists the held stance (wave-7 friction #1, still true). A
   bot that reads argument domains without reading `Available` is correct for
   the store and wrong for the stance, which is the worst possible shape for a
   convention.
3. **A one-tick guard windup has no observable "rising" state, and that is what
   makes the shell bait work.** The bulwark cohort's shell drops for exactly one
   tick, invites a bolt, and goes back up; because the entry windup is 1, the
   `form-transition-started` and `form-transition-completed` facts arrive on the
   same tick, so there is no observation in which "a guard is coming up" is true
   and the guard is not yet up. The only readable defence is evidence — the
   deflection count this body has already been seen to make — not the current
   form. Nothing in the brief points this out, and it is worth a sentence beside
   the shell's "the arc never tracks you, so going around it always works",
   because going around it requires knowing it will be there.
4. **The scaffold changed which random stream it draws from, and the default
   flipped.** `ArenaBasics.OrderedDirections` now takes an optional
   `IBotRandom` defaulting to `context.TeamRandom`, where the previous scaffold
   used `context.Random` unconditionally. A lineage that copies the new scaffold
   forward silently changes every equal-cost route tie-break from per-life to
   per-team. That is a real behaviour change and for this doctrine it is a
   **losing** one — measured at −3.28 ± 1.73 territory over 220 paired matches
   when I first adopted it, concentrated in the cell where two of my bodies
   route around the same obstacle. The XML doc explains the trap it fixes very
   well; it does not warn that adopting the fix is itself a behaviour change
   with a sign.
5. **`Position` has `ChebyshevDistance` and nothing else, on an experiment where
   every movement profile is four-way, one tile per tick.** Every schedule
   question the economy poses — "can I reach that deposit before it expires",
   "is the metronome close enough to start walking" — is a Manhattan question,
   and Chebyshev is the wrong answer to all of them. I wrote a four-line local
   helper; so will everybody else.
6. **`ImmutableArray<T>.Count` is a method group.** `context.Enemies.Count == 0`
   does not compile and the diagnostic is `Operator '==' cannot be applied to
   operands of type 'method group' and 'int'`, which does not say "you wanted
   `.Length`". Minor, but it is the first thing anyone writes.
7. **`--five-slots wane` is load-bearing and silent.** Omitting it in a
   fabricator pair produces a different, perfectly valid cell with no warning
   that the commissioned variant was not the one that ran. Same failure shape as
   wave-7 friction #5 (`--classes` being mandatory for `.wasm` specs, which is
   also still true).
8. **`qualify` still writes a viewer per probe replay.** 36 of them, 213 MB of
   evidence directory against 20 MB of actual evidence, on a run that emits no
   viewer anywhere else without `--viewer`/`--open`. Wave 7 reported this; it is
   unchanged, and this freeze prunes them again.
9. **Replay volume is still the real cost of a measured pass, and it is now the
   THROUGHPUT cost too.** A 500-tick replay v3 is 5–8 MB because it embeds the
   exact observation handed to every life on every tick, which is precisely what
   makes this analysis possible. But a sweep harness that parses those files
   in-process is bottlenecked on JSON parsing rather than on the engine: my
   first driver used a thread pool and ran at roughly a quarter of the speed of
   the identical driver on a process pool, because the parse holds the GIL while
   the matches themselves are subprocesses. Worth a line in the measurement
   guidance; it is not obvious that the analysis, not the simulation, is the
   long pole.
10. **The shared box is the largest single source of measurement wall-time
    variance, and nothing warns you.** Concurrent sibling authors took the same
    132-match sweep from 70 s to 20 minutes and back. Determinism means the
    numbers are unaffected, which is exactly why it is easy to mistake a
    contended machine for a hung one — I killed and restarted a sweep twice
    before diagnosing it.

## Carried-forward friction (still true from wave 7)

- Debug strings are part of the decision and therefore part of the replay hash.
- The qualification suite runs the duel-depth **union** profile, not the class
  arm, so a class-armed bot's T4 says nothing about its class play. Both were
  measured separately here.
- `--print-candidate-contract` emits the identity, not the resolved contract.
  The only way to read what a bot will actually see is to run a match and pull
  `header.contract` out of the replay, which is what I did to read the channel
  and economy blocks.
- A frozen tree's `ProjectReference` depth is load-bearing for `dotnet build`
  (not for `nilbots build`). My scratch tree is at the freeze depth for this
  reason.

## Method

**Metric.** Signed territorial delta (mine − theirs) per match, plus
win-loss-draw. Note the SCALE MOVED: the territorial formula is
`advance × (index − centre) × threshold + signed claim`, so a base breach
saturates at ±60 on a 15-threshold ruleset and at **±32** on the channel's
threshold of 8. A bastion or siege number is therefore not comparable to a
forge, swell, or wave-7 number without the 8/15 factor the brief names. Every
table below reports the cells separately for exactly that reason.

**Cells.** All four commissioned cells, spelled
`--classes <pair> --movement facing-locked --pendulum keel --skills kit --bend
universal --aim offset --stance-ground open --cooldown ticking --volley salvo`
plus `--capture channel` and/or `--economy scrap`, with `--five-slots wane`
added in the fabricator pair. Resolved identities:
`frontline-labs-1-striker-vs-striker-bastion-facing-locked` (rules fingerprint
`83c926ebd67a39337186c1fb177da6a5d10e5191e23d24f4e1c7497751364796`) and its
siege / forge / swell equivalents.

**Pairings.** 8 opponents × 3 seeds × 4 cells. Same-class cells (the three
strikers, one of which is my own wave-7 self) are played from **both team
sides**, because a mirror-symmetric map carries a team-slot bias large enough to
be mistaken for a result. 132 matches per configuration.

**Seeds are not observations.** Both policies are deterministic; the seed feeds
`context.Random`, which this lineage uses only to order equal-scoring
directions, and `context.TeamRandom`, which it uses only for the lane draw.
Seeds therefore produce distinct *replays* and frequently identical *outcomes*.
Disclosed for the shipped configuration over its 132 matches: **65 distinct
(cell, opponent, outcome, territory) tuples and 82 distinct tuples once the team
side, stance entries and purchases are included.** Replay-hash distinctness is
NOT disclosed as evidence — see platform friction #1, it is 132/132 by
construction.

**Statistic.** Every configuration plays the identical 132 pairings, so all
comparisons are **paired**: the reported number is the mean per-pairing
difference in territorial delta with its standard error over the 132 pairings,
and the count of pairings whose result actually changed.

**Search versus attribution.** Candidates were found by screening, by tracing
single replays tick by tick, and by bisecting a regression down to one switch on
six matches at a time. The **reported attribution is leave-one-out from the
shipped whole**; rules that are not shipped are reported as add-one-in cost
against that same whole.

**The null is exact.** With every wave-8 switch off, the artifact reproduces the
wave-7 result on all 220 pairings of the wider screening set — identical
outcome, identical territory, identical stance entries, identical casts, 0
differing. The switch scaffolding is a faithful identity, so every difference
below is attributable to a rule rather than to the refactor.

**The mid-wave engine republish is verified inert for this cell set.** The
toolchain was republished at 01:38 while my attribution round was running, which
changed the staged `BotArena.Sdk`/`BotArena.Guest` bytes and therefore every
artifact hash (they are compiled into each artifact and feed the build-cache
key). Two checks were run rather than assumed: (a) the same artifact replayed on
the new engine reproduces a stored replay hash exactly (`6696b618…`, 435 of 435
decisions identical); (b) a variant built BEFORE the republish, rebuilt after it
into different artifact bytes (`53eb1836…` → `f7317d80…`), reproduces all 36
sampled recorded rows exactly. No match in any sweep aborted: every sweep
recorded a complete row set from a complete replay, and a missing replay would
have raised rather than scored. **The edge track settled in exactly one match
across the whole study, and that match ran on the FIXED engine.** Every purchase
in the 132-match attribution set was `optic`; on the 220-match disjoint-seed
confirmation set, run after the republish, the ladder reached a third tier once
and bought `edge` — no abort, complete replay, scored normally. So the defect
could not have been triggered by any measurement reported here, and there is no
workaround to remove: `edge` is ranked second on merit by the effect valuation
(this chassis's sight-to-reach gap is two tiers wide and its travel margin over
the opposing catalog already exists), not avoided.

### Reading of each number

- **`StillClaim` is the pass**, at +5.18 ± 1.51 overall and **+18.42 ± 5.15 in
  the bastion cell alone**, on 15 of its 33 pairings. It is also the only rule
  in this doctrine's history that pays a body for *not* moving, and the reason
  it pays is arithmetic rather than temperament: claim weight counts only bodies
  whose tile did not change, so a doctrine that shuffles on the point captures
  nothing at all. Its per-class split (striker +14.50, bulwark +8.00, fabricator
  +1.50) says the same thing from the other side — it is worth most where the
  fight is body-for-body and least where the opponent can simply out-count me.
- **The economy is a PACKAGE and must be priced as one.** `Invest`
  (−0.41 ± 0.46) and `Intercept` (−0.32 ± 0.32) each measure slightly negative
  alone, and removing all four economy rules together costs **+1.85 ± 1.01**,
  +7.64 ± 3.67 of it in the bastion cell. That is not a contradiction: the
  collect side is worth nothing without the spend side, so a leave-one-out that
  removes the spend side leaves the walking cost and deletes the payoff. I ship
  all four and report both numbers rather than quoting the flattering one.
- **`Assay` carries +1.41 ± 0.75 on 8 pairings and is entirely a bastion
  effect.** It is also the rule that produced this pass's sharpest contract
  reading. Ungated, it cost about 26 territory a match against the fabricator
  cohort on the `forge` cell (5-1 became 0-6 on the same seeds), because a
  one-scrap wreck was pulling a body off a point whose capture rate scales with
  net weight. Gated on `stationaryGainMultiplierCap` — the field that says the
  marginal body has stopped paying — it costs nothing there and pays where the
  cap exists. The gate is contract data, not a cell name.
- **`Interrupt` measures exactly zero on 0 of 132 pairings, and it is still the
  doctrine's account of the arm.** The mechanic itself is doing an enormous
  amount of work: over the 33 bastion matches the shipped artifact lands **316
  interrupt-scope contacts for 318 points of reverted enemy progress** against
  105 taken, which at a threshold of 8 is roughly forty captures' worth of
  denial. What measures zero is the explicit RULE, because the wave-5 shot
  ladder already fires at exactly those bodies: an interrupt target is anchored
  (+1.5) and standing on an objective tile (+0.4), which clears the 2.5 fire bar
  before the interrupt bonus is added. Sampled on the deck replay: 22 ticks with
  a ready gun, a controlling enemy on the point, and my body off it; the bot
  fired on 9 and spent the other 13 acquiring a lane it did not have. I ship the
  rule as a belt on braces and as a hedge against a contract whose anchored or
  objective bonus is smaller, and I do not claim it as a win.
- **`Screen` also measures exactly zero, and the reason is opportunity, not
  correctness.** The escort geometry needs three things at once: an ally
  channelling on the point, me off it, and an enemy on a clear firing line to
  that ally. Measured on the deck replay: 229 of my life-ticks, 27 with an ally
  on the point, 5 with me off it and an enemy visible. Three slots and an
  18-tick respawn do not produce escorts often enough for the rule to bind. It
  is shipped because the arithmetic is right and it costs nothing; it is not
  reported as a win, and I would expect it to matter in a five-slot arm.
- **`MirrorRefusal` is +0.39 ± 0.89 overall and the number hides its shape:**
  +4.36 ± 2.44 on bastion, −2.79 ± 2.51 on siege, 7 pairings changed in total.
  It is shipped on the contract argument rather than the point estimate — a bolt
  that arrives inside a declared projectile guard's quadrant comes back from
  that tile carrying its own damage class, so firing it is a self-inflicted hit
  and no prediction weight further down the lane can be worth one. Wave 7 priced
  it as a graded penalty and I have the replay where that penalty lost: three
  bolts into an aegis shell down a straight corridor, dead on tick 16, the
  objective free for the rest of the match. The rule fixes that specific
  suicide. It does not fix the bulwark's *bait* — dropping the shield for one
  tick to invite a shot — and I did not have budget to add the evidence-based
  answer to that.
- **`DenyPresence` is +0.65 ± 0.75, and the interesting half is the bulwark
  cell at +8.44 ± 4.30.** Denial counting every body, moving or not, is exactly
  what a lone striker needs against a chassis that out-healths it: standing in
  the region at all nulls a stationary bulwark, and this lineage has never
  before taken a match off a bulwark in any wave. It takes three here.
- **`ErosionClock` (−0.06 ± 0.06 on one pairing) and `Intercept`
  (−0.32 ± 0.32 on one pairing) are shipped as corrections, not as wins.** The
  first fixes a budget that was wrong by the declared erosion multiple; the
  second is one line in the shot valuation that reads a published field. Both
  measure indistinguishably from zero and both are reported as such.
- **The three wave-7 rejects stay rejected, and one of them was expected to
  flip.** `CheapEntry` was the one with a mechanism-level reason: it is the
  clause that refuses a cast from any tile a loaded enemy muzzle could cover,
  which is precisely the tile a channelling opponent is covering, so I expected
  the re-armed fan's new territorial value to pay for the exposure. It does not
  — +1.91 ± 2.38 to leave it off, on 60 changed pairings, and its per-cell split
  (+7.27 bastion, −3.76 siege) is a coin. `HedgeFan` is the most expensive at
  +4.89 ± 2.93, and it is worse against fabricators (−14.33) than anything else
  this lineage has measured.
- **The scaffold's new team-safe tie-break costs +2.15 ± 1.75 on 29 pairings and
  is NOT shipped.** This is the one rule where the honest answer disagrees with
  the obvious one. `TeamRandom` genuinely fixes the trap its documentation
  describes — teammates silently disagreeing about a shared plan — but this
  doctrine's convoy layer already resolves right-of-way from the frozen
  observation, and giving every body the same route tie-break makes them
  contend for the same tile before that layer can separate them. The per-life
  stream was doing useful work by accident. `TeamRandom` IS used, for the one
  thing it is for: the courier's lane draw, where the whole team must agree and
  the opponent must not be able to predict.

## What did not work and why it is worth recording

1. **I gated a rule on the wrong thing twice before finding the field that
   actually decides it.** The economy's cost is a body's absence from the point,
   and my first two attempts to price that were a posture test and then a
   two-live-bodies test. Both are proxies. The thing that decides it is
   `stationaryGainMultiplierCap`: where surplus past a ceiling buys no capture
   speed, the marginal body is free; where net weight scales the rate, it is
   never free. Getting that wrong cost the fabricator cell 26 territory a match
   on the `forge` cell and I found it by bisecting one match's decisions down to
   a single switch, not by sweeping.
2. **A rule can be correct, necessary, and measure zero, and the honest thing is
   to say which.** `Interrupt` and `Screen` both measure exactly zero on 0 of
   132 pairings, for two entirely different reasons — the first because the
   ladder it was added to already produced the behaviour, the second because the
   situation does not arise at three slots. I report the mechanic's usage
   statistics (316 interrupt contacts, 318 reverted progress, per 33 bastion
   matches) beside the rule's attribution (zero) rather than letting the first
   stand in for the second.
3. **The most valuable single change in this pass is not about either new
   arm.** It is refusing to fire into a projectile guard. Wave 7 had that rule
   as a score penalty and the penalty lost to a large enough prediction; the
   measured consequence was a body killing itself with its own three bolts and
   handing over the objective. I would not have found it from a sweep — I found
   it by reading one lost match tick by tick, which is the thing the replay
   format makes possible and the aggregate does not.
4. **My own harness was the measurement bottleneck and briefly the measurement
   hazard.** A thread pool parsing multi-megabyte replays ran at a quarter of
   the throughput of a process pool; a shell glob that failed silently left a
   stale sweep file that a "cached" check would have read as current; and two
   attribution rounds had to be discarded because I changed the source under a
   running sweep. The last one is the real lesson: freeze the tree, then
   attribute.

Two things worth stating plainly.

**The bulwark result replicates exactly and it is the first this lineage has
had.** Wave 7 lost 60 of 60 bulwark matches in every one of fourteen measured
configurations and declared the cell out of budget. On the channel this
revision takes 3 of 9 and 5 of 15, at +16.00 on both seed sets. The mechanism is
not subtle: a bulwark's capture needs stationary claim weight, denial counts
every body of mine whether it moves or not, and the aegis shell no longer gets
free damage out of my own gun.

**The fabricator cell is the pass's honest loss, and it is a REGRESSION on this
arm.** Wave 7 beat the fabricators 9-1 on `swell`; this revision still does
(8-2 on the disjoint set, unchanged, because nothing it added touches that
cell) — but under the channel both revisions lose the cell outright, and mine
loses it by more (−6.4 to −10.7 against wave 7's already-losing number). The
reason is the cap: a five-slot fabricator reaches the stationary surplus
ceiling with bodies to spare, and every rule I added that makes a body worth
more on the point makes the body I do not have hurt more. I do not have a fix
inside this budget and I am not going to pretend the cap gate was one.
