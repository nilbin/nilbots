nilbots — logotype, second pass

A construction, not an icon

The first attempt swapped a letter for a target ring. That is the most-worn move in the category — every robotics, esports and shooter brand has spent it — and it said nothing about this game in particular. This one is built rather than decorated: the wordmark is drawn on the arena's own grid, and the same tiles render as a path, a wall, or a floor.

Two things were wrong with the reticle mark, and they were the same thing twice.

Replacing the o with a ring is stock, and setting nil light against bots heavy is a typographic default rather than an idea. Neither was derived from anything nilbots actually does.

The construction

One grid, one alphabet, no drawing by hand

The arena is tiles, and a tile is either wall or floor. So the letters are polylines through tile centres, right angles only, because that is how a bot moves. Rasterise the polyline and you have the tiles. Trace the tiles and you have the outline. Round every corner — convex and concave alike, at the radius the renderer chamfers walls with — and the letterform is finished. Every mark on this page comes out of those four steps, which is why they all fit each other exactly and why any of them can be regenerated at any size.

Two decisions worth naming. The t's crossbar runs twice as far right as left and the stem turns out at the foot — a symmetrical one reads as a dagger. And the i's tittle is the only tile in the word touching nothing else, which makes it the natural place to put state: the point the pen starts from, the dot that pulses while a match loads.

Three finishes

Path, wall, floor — the arena's three primitives, from one set of tiles

Nothing is redrawn between them. The trace is the skeleton stroked one tile wide; the matter is those tiles filled; the plan is those same tiles cut out of a slab.

Ground

nil is the empty tile; bots is what stands on it

One variant does something the finishes can't do alone: it splits the word across the wall/floor line. nil is cut out of the block — it is the absence. bots is solid, standing on the ground. Same grid, same letters, no second colour, no letter swapped for a picture. It is the one mark here that says something about the name.

At size

Where it stops working, and what to use instead

The top bar sets it around 22px, a mobile header around 18. Matter is still clean at 15. At 12 the counters in b and o are about three pixels wide on a 1× display, which survives here but will not survive being rendered under a shadow or over a texture — that is where the mark takes over.

The mark

An n, which is also a corridor

Cut out of a wall block, the n is a U-turn passage — a fragment of arena that happens to be the first letter. The block is the icon's own tile, so iOS's corner mask finishes it rather than fighting it; the letter sits far enough in to survive the mask.

Colour

It stays out of the player's way

Accent and chassis are two independent picks a player makes, from a free colour input — so no hue can be reserved for the brand. The logotype answers that by having no hue at all: one path, currentColor, no gradient and no second colour anywhere in the system. Whatever colour it takes, it took from its surroundings: on the Forge ground that is the brand's burnt orange #c8763c, and on a white page it is the ink. The mark never chooses — the surface does.

How to use it

One geometry, four jobs

  • WordmarkMatter. Top bar, headers, docs, the CLI's own banner. Chamfered ends keep it in the renderer's vocabulary, and it is the finish that survives smallest.
  • IconPlan mark. Favicon, app icon, avatar, anywhere square.
  • MotionTrace. The wordmark drawing itself on first load, and the same stroke as the loading indicator — nothing else needs a spinner.
  • EmphaticGround. Launcher, season card, merchandise. Never under 90px, where the knocked-out nil closes up.
  • Clear spaceOne tile on every side — the same tile the letters are built from.
  • TypeThe logotype has no typeface — it has a grid. What it sits beside is Archivo, one variable family used at three widths, with mono kept for values a machine wrote. That system is specified on the Climb page.
  • NeverColour part of the word differently, outline it, put it on a photograph, or set it in a real typeface. It has no typeface; it has a grid.

What it replaces

Three files, and one thing that isn't decided

web/src/components/Logo.tsx currently sets two spans and hard-codes #38bdf8 on the word bots; its mobile counterpart does the same; the favicon in index.html is a third copy of that blue. Each becomes one <svg> holding a single <path fill="currentColor"> — under a kilobyte, no font dependency, and correct in both themes without a second asset.

The open item: the plan finish is a tile layout, but not a connected one. The letters are separate chambers, so as a map it is unplayable. Opening the gaps at the baseline would fix that and make the logotype an arena you could run a match in — which is a loading screen, or an April joke, and not the logo. Worth knowing it's a tile edit away.
