## 1. Frozen Vanguard

The reference is the exact persisted editor artifact from revision 22, not a hand-reconstructed approximation. Its sheet and map plan are now immutable inputs for later counter/counter-counter work.

Tracing corrected an important premise before counter authoring: Vanguard is not principally a home-invasion rush. It establishes home and centre, overloads the enemy-side Well with supported Towlines, sends a Kestrel through a northern ingress, and converges on globally known loose Cores. Counter designs aimed at a fictional eight-body home siege were discarded.

## 2. Counterfort

### Observable recognition

Entry requires both:

- at least one **visible enemy carrier**; and
- at least one **recently remembered enemy** in `enemy-score-corridor`, with an explicit 45-tick freshness horizon.

No sheet identity, intended route, hidden position, or eventual outcome is available to the rule. It reacts only to information the mind legitimately sees or remembers.

When both facts hold, the four far-side bodies enter `collapse-return-lane`: Towline pulls, Sunder marks, Hush suppresses, then the group focuses the carrier and closes the corridor. When either fact disappears, ordered modes naturally fall back to loose-Core work, recovery, ordinary combat, and enemy-Well occupation.

## Does the DSL have enough strategic vocabulary?

For this ladder, yes. No compiler, executor, engine, fact, operator, or schema extension was needed.

The useful combination was:

- ordered and nested modes for explicit priority and fallback;
- `visible-enemy-carriers` for immediate evidence;
- zone-scoped remembered-enemy counts with authored freshness horizons;
- separate entry/exit thresholds for hysteresis;
- `self-in-zone` plus `use.at`/authored anchors for signature setup;
- shared capacities for loose-Core contention.

Memory is deliberately short-lived, so every recognition rule here says how fresh evidence must be. A generic `forTicks` qualifier may eventually improve sustained-pattern recognition, but these examples did not justify adding it: the entry/exit boundaries were expressible and observable without it.

One concrete residual limitation appeared in discarded strict-lane experiments: a condition can count known loose Cores in a zone, but `move.to: nearest-known-loose-core` cannot constrain its chosen Core to that same zone. That makes a zone-gated collector capable of pursuing a closer remembered Core elsewhere. This did not block the four delivered strategies, so it is reported rather than expanded into an unproven selector feature.

## Discarded paths and lessons

- The first counter premise targeted a home-invasion behavior Vanguard did not actually exhibit. Replay traces and spatial reads invalidated it.
- A generic centre-mass collapse was mostly “fight harder,” not recognition-driven counterplay, and varied strongly by side.
- Longshot and Mortar trials introduced facing-locked rotation as a confound, so the final counter uses signatures whose intended response was legible in the exhibit.
- Several gate-anchor variants overcommitted to geometry while missing live Core traffic.
- Foundry Net's first Nest route proved that static signature intent still needs a valid ingress; this was fixed in the sheet rather than hidden in the executor.

## Gallery and tooling integrity

Curated gallery cards name the actual opponent, final score, trigger, intended tactic, counterplay, fallback, and the tick window to watch. A tooling defect was found while regenerating them: every nested reference sheet is named `orders.json`, so the exhibit script's basename substring match could attribute the entrant to the wrong participant. The script now resolves the exact sheet path and reads the authored `sheetId`; the final cards were rebuilt after that correction.

The three galleries are served at site roots because the production viewer intentionally uses root-relative asset URLs:
