# Arc Relay doctrine expansion — 2026-08-01

## RESULT

Arc Relay now has twelve evaluation-grade doctrines driven by one frozen WASM
algorithm and twelve independently hashed sheets. The original five controls
remain byte-for-byte unchanged: Balanced, Control Grid, Convoy, Interception,
and Split. Seven additions exercise mechanics and spatial plans that the small
cohort was not demonstrating:

- Relay Chain stages receivers and uses Arc Toss or emergency Exchange;
- Fortress Counterattack builds forward protection before a swift return;
- Trap Punish seeds public routes and layers delayed area fire;
- Fireline Picks creates revealed firing corridors and protects its backline;
- Displacement Control attacks carrier geometry with pulls, bursts, and swaps;
- Sustain Attrition layers repair, walls, sentries, and suppression; and
- Feint Switch overloads one outer theater before a scheduled inversion.

No rules, class statistics, map geometry, objective cadence, economy, comeback
mechanic, or score-to-power rule changed. The approved eight-body sheet model
remains intact: any unlocked classes, duplicate classes under the existing slot
rules, and no stable-of-five structure.

The full mirrored round robin completed 132/132 eligible WASM matches with
132/132 exact replay verifications and zero retained canonical scratch files.
The result demonstrates evaluation depth: doctrine shapes differ materially,
every intended signature family was exercised, and several strong aggregate
doctrines have specific losing matchups. It also rejects the stronger
pre-registered hypothesis that this pack is already grossly balanced. Three
doctrines finished 16-6 while Convoy finished 4-18, Relay Chain 5-3-14, and
Fireline Picks 6-2-14. Class-stat tuning from these results is forbidden; the
remaining spread is doctrine implementation evidence, not a class verdict.

First-Pulse conversion remains an explicit concern. It was 110/132 (`83.3%`)
in the full cohort and 12/12 in the frozen review sample, both above the
registered 70% alert. The gallery therefore tests whether the expanded tactical
story is visible despite the unresolved initiative bias; it is not evidence
that the initiative problem is solved.

The sheet format remains **provisional evaluation data**. It exists to cover
the roster, hash inputs reproducibly, and produce comparable replays. It is not
the player-facing sheet schema, drawing/editing UX, or unlock design.

### 4. Eligibility repairs and rejected tuning

Two deterministic behavior repairs were required before the final read:

- a carrier that had made no progress for eight ticks now drops the Core before
  issuing another blocked movement request, so an accepted-but-unresolved move
  cannot starve the drop fallback; and
- patrol phase is staggered by unit identity, preventing all eight bodies from
  synchronizing into a formation freeze.

Both are evaluation-mind corrections, not game-rule changes. The final cohort
has 132 retained cells, zero excluded cells, and no ineligible entrants.

One post-read, one-factor attempt changed Convoy to prefer its assigned theater.
The registered eight-cell target screen went 0-8 and reduced objective tempo,
so it was rejected and the byte-frozen Convoy sheet restored. This avoids
turning a failed result into hidden class-stat churn. Convoy remains a known
weak doctrine in this pack.

### 5. Engine correctness found by the broader behavior surface

The expansion exposed two legal action sequences that previously aborted the
engine:

1. Ordered signature damage followed by Repair Beam could return a life to its
   exact tick-start state. Chronology validation rejected the unchanged endpoint
   merely because events existed. It now replays the ordered Arc facts and
   accepts an endpoint only when those facts explain it. The exact reproduction
   verifies as canonical SHA-256
   `b1c980d4d49f4275adccc334a54d2217990c92025c80690937954bf68df0a2fb`.
2. A Survey Flare owner could be displaced onto the flare target after legal
   aim but before activation, creating duplicate endpoint geometry. Coincident
   endpoints are now canonicalized to one position for the four endpoint-based
   signatures. The exact reproduction verifies as canonical SHA-256
   `bdd72c311c7fc4c98c7ec5e0762f9d1f2a08be036df11e22410ba337ab7bbe04`.

Both paths have focused regressions. Normal distinct-endpoint behavior and the
frozen golden replays remain unchanged.
