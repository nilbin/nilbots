## Shared gates

1. **Concept gate:** immutable approved identity and explicit form/material
   authority are named and hashed.
2. **Provider gate:** the request and credit boundary are pinned; provider
   results are labeled experimental.
3. **Blender gate:** orientation, scale, topology, materials, pivots, anchors,
   and animation ownership become deterministic.
4. **Runtime gate:** the asset is loaded in the actual renderer at actual
   gameplay distance, team states, camera limits, and map clearance.
5. **Review gate:** owner comparison uses matched frames rather than provider
   thumbnails.
6. **Validation gate:** GLB, geometry, PBR, semantic accents, animation,
   collision/cover clearance, bytes, loading, and performance all have recorded
   evidence.
7. **Decision gate:** explicitly accept, iterate, switch path, or retain the
   control. No branch-local decision number is minted.

## Proven T2-to-rigid-animation slice

The B1 result narrows the earlier pipeline uncertainty:

- Meshy T2 supplied the approved visual/PBR surface but no names, pivots, skin,
  or clips.
- Exact-position welded connectivity exposed 206 real loose components behind
  2,529 indexed seam islands.
- A deterministic Blender recipe assigned every component once to a hub or one
  of four radial arms. It retained the original triangle-position/UV multiset
  exactly and left the provider atlas untouched.
- Four removable `team-accent` strips and one fixed `class-energy` ring were
  added as independent geometry rather than recoloring the fused atlas.
- A five-joint, one-weight-per-vertex skin keeps the hard surface rigid.
  `Turret_Deploy` and `Turret_Recoil` move only the radial locks; they do not
  scan, turn, or imply a new firing direction.
- Two independent Blender processes produced byte-identical grouped and
  animated GLBs plus byte-identical reports. Both GLBs have zero Khronos
  validator findings.

This is a viable automated path when measured component grouping is
unambiguous. It is not a blanket promise: a fused manifold, cross-arm
ambiguity, missing visual mass, or untrustworthy mechanical seam must stop at
the topology gate and proceed through functional retopology instead.

## Rejected shortcuts

Meshy Auto Split is not a production path: automatic parts cannot be trusted
to encode the exact hub, four equal arms, locks, team inlays, pivots, and recoil
contract.

Meshy vehicle auto-rig is also rejected: a generic vehicle skeleton does not
describe radial deployment, planted locks, recoil, the fire anchor, or the
whole-object mount. If a provider result is used, Blender owns all functional
segmentation and animation deterministically.
