## Shared generation contract

Every call requested exactly one rigid standalone attachment, shown as the same
object in four equal panels: strict top at upper left, strict side at upper
right, front three-quarter at lower left, and rear three-quarter at lower right.
Objects were centered, equally scaled, fully visible, and isolated on a uniform
light neutral-gray background with thin white gutters, diffuse neutral light,
and no floor, horizon, cast shadow, labels, arrows, or text.

The shared material brief was a stylized hard-surface game asset with broad
readable planes, chunky chamfers, rough/matte desaturated aged warm brass or
bronze, graphite only in recesses/underside, restrained narrow amber emissive
inlays, and sparse edge wear. Every call excluded glossy plastic, chrome,
lacquer, team teal/cyan, saturated orange team panels, strong blue/red/white,
bot bodies, chassis, weapons, guns, barrels, eyes, legs, cables, floating parts,
letters, numbers, rank symbols, stars, pedestals, and scenery.

## Per-module geometry briefs

- `veterancy-crown-spine`: compact centered dorsal crest with a clipped
  longitudinal planform, one low stepped rise-and-fall rhythm, two separated
  underside mounting pads, and no lateral wings. Two rejected drafts are
  retained because they became aircraft-like and then too long/flat.
- `veterancy-swept-blade`: broad, low, rear-swept armor blade with a narrow
  clipped forward end, flared rear cap, recessed inner seam, and flat mounting
  underside; more armored fin than wing, sword, or gun.
- `veterancy-square-mantle`: compact stepped squared shoulder plate with an open
  notched inner edge and enough negative space to avoid a solid box.
- `veterancy-radial-petal`: low mechanical wedge with a narrow inner root and
  broad clipped outer cap, intended to rotate around Y with visible gaps; never
  an organic flower, shield, or orbit ring.
- `veterancy-curved-rail`: one thick low 100–120-degree broken mantle segment
  with capped ends and a dominant open inner void; never a complete halo,
  horseshoe, or force-field hoop.
