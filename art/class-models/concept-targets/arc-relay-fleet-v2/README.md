# Arc Relay fleet conceptual 3D v2

- Status: concept review only. No Meshy task has been submitted.
- Purpose: brighter, mechanically sophisticated image-to-3D targets for a
  possible second Arc Relay fleet pass. The v5 direction explicitly rejects
  geometry simplification as a route to gameplay readability.
- Canonical gameplay identity remains the sixteen `arc-*` class looks. These
  concepts may clarify depth and simplify finish, but they may not change a
  class's rule-bearing hardware or promise a new capability.
- Model-owned team color is deliberately absent. Team ownership remains a

The generated sheets keep the exact class count/order and clear the intended
payload distinction: Minesmith carries flat marked discs, while Nest carries
two barrelled turret pods. The selected pass restores class-specific fixed
paint without adding semantic team color: each chassis keeps the shared bright
ivory/metal family, while 25–40% of its large armor panels carry one muted
ochre, olive, orange, indigo, violet, bronze, or tan class color. Emissives stay
neutral amber. Its soft studio contact shadows are acceptable for concept
review but are not approved provider inputs. If the direction is approved,
isolation must remove the shadows and produce one clean square image of at
least 1040 pixels per bot before any Meshy request.

The high-chroma experiment increases the fixed paint coverage to roughly
45–60% and uses saturated yellow, green, orange, turquoise, violet, blue,
magenta, red, purple, and lime. It has a materially stronger 63-pixel read, but
some fixed hues necessarily overlap the team-color spectrum. Emissives remain
neutral; approval still requires a real-replay check that renderer-owned team
ownership remains faster to read than class paint.

The industrial-color refinement keeps the same clear hue assignments while
reducing paint coverage to roughly 30–45%, deepening the pigments, replacing
pure white with warm coated metal, and returning all functional hardware to
graphite, steel, rubber, or bronze. Restrained abrasion and roughness variation
remove the molded-plastic/toy read without restoring the original fleet's dark
muddy value structure.

The complex-industrial v5 pass reverses the earlier simplification direction.
It retains the original concept roster's layered armor, mechanisms, suspension,
negative spaces, and secondary construction while moving clear class paint onto
large satin-enamel panels. At the 63-pixel proof, silhouettes and fixed colors
remain distinct without relying on black outlines. Minesmith now reads as three
flat mines in a detailed feed rack. Nest remains below the provider-input gate:
the carrier is appropriately sophisticated, but its two folded sentry assemblies
need a clearer omnidirectional deployment read before any paid Meshy call.

The v6 value correction keeps the v5 geometry and studio exposure fixed while
lifting broad armor on Hush, Relay, Switchback, Longshot, Veil, Nest, Mortar,
and Repulsor. It confines near-black values to underbodies, joints, tread gaps,
bores, vents, and narrow recesses. This is the current concept candidate. Nest's
stored sentry hardware remains the next targeted identity correction.

The v7 redesign removes the road-vehicle fleet language. Twelve classes use
low ground-skimming hover bodies with a visible air gap and recessed field
hardware; Palisade, Mortar, Mason, and Nest alone use tracks. The silhouette
contract gives every class a different outer contour and negative space before
color. At the 63-pixel proof, the split arrow, shield block, winch tug, repair
cross, dish crescent, artillery tank, mine hex, oval band, U fork, diamond
frame, rail needle, rotary tool tank, mast delta, emitter ring, canister
catamaran, and sentry carrier remain separately identifiable.

The v8 surface pass retains those sixteen silhouettes and functional assemblies
while removing the high-contrast seam network. Broad enamel and pale-metal
planes now carry the value read; dark values are confined to actual cavities,
joints, bores, underbodies, and track gaps. The 63-pixel proof preserves the
class distinctions without reducing them to black-lined plate mosaics. It
failed the locomotion-read gate because several nominal hovers still appeared
to rest directly on their studio shadows.

The v9 physical pass raises the twelve intended hover chassis onto visible
underside field hardware and separates their ground shadows. Palisade, Mortar,
Mason, and Nest alone retain weight-bearing tracks and contact shadows. The
detached air gap and warm-neutral underbody cue survive the 63-pixel proof;
however, Patchbay's medical cross became too small and the roster repeated the
open twin-tip nose language across several classes.

The v10 identity pass restores one large top-facing white medical cross to
Patchbay and reserves an open twin-prong front for Relay alone. Kestrel now has
a single lance point, Lantern a single teardrop nose, Switchback a closed
reversal frame, and Veil a single smoke-layer wedge. These distinctions and the
cross survive the 63-pixel proof.

The v12 pass makes all sixteen bodies visibly levitate. The four formerly
tracked machines retain heavy bastion, artillery, workshop, and carrier
proportions on distinct grav platforms. It also removes Patchbay's redundant
raised arm, reserves the crane/hook silhouette for Towline, replaces Mason's
long booms with compact radial tools, and removes track-like skirt grids. This
pass still used warm lift pools, and its generic carried discs/pods did not
match the deployed signature props closely enough.

The v13 payload-fidelity pass removes all underbody emission and relies only on
air gaps plus detached neutral shadows. Minesmith now carries three folded Trip
Nodes with the approved three-prong graphite puck and triangular sensor
language. Nest carries two folded Sentinel Seeds with the approved taupe base,
yaw bearing, compact gun head, sensor slit, and short barrel. This is the
correct geometry, but the carried sentries remained too close to Nest's olive
carrier color.

The v14 material correction restores the deployed Sentinel Seed's noticeably
lighter warm sandstone-taupe ceramic over dark gunmetal bearings and barrels,
with restrained bronze hardware. The two payloads now remain visibly separate
from the olive/khaki Nest carrier at the 63-pixel proof.

The v15 drone-family pass retains Kestrel as an interceptor while redesigning
Lantern as an asymmetric tripod survey drone, Sunder as a compact X-planform
designator drone, and Veil as a continuous smoke-laying drone. Each uses a
different planform and enclosed lift-node grammar while remaining low-hover,
tile-bound, and non-emissive underneath. The enclosed lift nodes remained too
close to armored hover pucks to read as drones.

The v18 correction uses explicit UAV construction: Kestrel is fixed-wing with
a rear ducted pusher; Lantern has three open rotors; Sunder has four; Veil has
six. Visible blades, structural booms, and gimbals survive the 63-pixel proof.
The same proof also clears sixteen separate class-color identities; Repulsor's
cool silver body removes the last warm-palette collision. The literal copter
rings did not fit the fleet's established hard-surface language.

The v19 correction removes all exposed rotors. Kestrel remains a cockpitless
flying-wing interceptor UAV; Lantern becomes a twin-boom survey UAV with dish
and gimbal. Sunder and Veil return to solid designator and smoke-layer hover
bodies. The full sixteen-color pass is unchanged.

The v20 correction restores the canonical East/right presentation after the
v19 redesign. Kestrel and Sunder have an unambiguous right-side nose, Lantern's
camera nose sits right of its left-side H-tail, and Veil places its shallow prow
and forward sensor on the right with smoke hardware aft. Mortar and Longshot
remain the orientation anchors. This is the current concept candidate.
